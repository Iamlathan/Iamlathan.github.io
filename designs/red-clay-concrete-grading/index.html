Why the ground matters

A slab is only as good as the dirt under it

Hall County sits in the upper Piedmont, where the ground under the topsoil is often red clay. That red ground gave us our name, and it is what we grade and pour on.

  • We walk it first

    Slope, soil and how the trucks get in all change the job, so we look before we plan.

  • The water has somewhere to go

    We plan the grade and where the water runs off before the forms go down.

  • Ground and slab, one call

    The crew that grades the lot also pours the slab. You do not chase two crews.

Before the trucks

Get your site ready for us

A few things you can do before the site walk, so the visit goes quickly.

Before we come

  • Mark the spot. A few stakes or a line of spray paint shows the size and shape you have in mind.
  • Clear the way in. Move cars, trailers and anything stored along the path a truck will take.
  • Find the wet spots. Severe storms come through here from March to November. Note where water stands after one, or take a photo.
  • Get a rough size. Length and width in feet, and what is going to sit on it.
  • Find the site plan. Building a house pad? Keep the site plan handy, if there is one.
  • Think about the truck. Note whether a mixer truck can back up to the spot.

After the pour, stay off it until we tell you it is ready.

Questions

Before the first truck

Do you need to see the site?

Yes. We walk every site before we price it.

How thick should a driveway be?

Most home driveways are poured about 4 inches thick. Heavier trucks call for more. We settle it on the walk.

When can I drive on it?

Give it about a week for a car and longer for anything heavy. We will tell you for your pour.

Why does water keep standing in my yard?

Tell us where it sits after a rain. Sometimes the fix is in the grade of the yard, not a new drain.
